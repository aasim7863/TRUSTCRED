The architecture

From issuance to verification, in five steps

Every credential moves through the same fixed sequence — the ordering is what makes the tamper-evidence work: nothing downstream can be trusted unless everything upstream is intact.

1

Issue

The institution issues a digital credential record containing the student's achievement data and issuer signature.

2

Hash

The credential content is cryptographically hashed — a fingerprint that changes completely if a single character is altered.

3

Anchor on-chain

Only the hash (not the raw credential data) is written to the ledger, timestamped and linked to the previous block.

4

Share selectively

The student shares the credential with an employer directly — the chain never stores or exposes the underlying personal data.

5

Verify

The employer re-hashes the received credential and compares it against the on-chain record — any mismatch means tampering.

What it does

Built around one core guarantee

Every design decision traces back to a single question: can the record be trusted without trusting a middleman?

🔗

Tamper-proof record integrity

Because each block references the hash of the one before it, altering any past credential record would break the chain — making tampering mathematically detectable, not just policy-forbidden.

🕶️

Privacy-preserving verification

The ledger stores only cryptographic hashes, never raw academic records — so verification can happen without exposing grades, personal identifiers, or institutional data publicly.

🏛️

Decentralized trust model

No single institution or intermediary needs to be contacted to confirm authenticity — verification is possible directly against the shared ledger.

🎓

Designed for three stakeholders

The system is architected around the actual verification triangle — students who own their credentials, institutions who issue them, and employers who need to trust them quickly.

Who it's for

Three sides of the same problem

Credential fraud and slow verification cost time and trust for everyone in the chain — TrustCred is designed to shorten that chain.

🎓

Students

Own a verifiable, portable record of their credentials that they control and share directly.

  • Share credentials without waiting on registrar processing
  • Keep control over who sees underlying academic data
🏢

Employers

Confirm a candidate's credential is genuine without a manual verification request to the institution.

  • Instant hash-based authenticity check
  • Lower risk of accepting fabricated qualifications
🏛️

Institutions

Issue credentials once, with tamper-evidence built in, instead of fielding repeated manual verification requests.

  • Reduce verification workload on registrar staff
  • Strengthen institutional credibility against forged records
